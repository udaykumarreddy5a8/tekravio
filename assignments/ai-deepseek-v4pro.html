// The Context
DeepSeek V4 Pro continues to disrupt the intelligence-per-dollar ratio of the frontier AI market. Released April 24, 2026 with a MIT open-source license and weights publicly available on Hugging Face — the most permissive licence at frontier capability. The architecture uses Mixture-of-Experts with 680B total parameters, activating only 42B per token, enabled by Multi-head Latent Attention (MLA) which achieves an 88% reduction in inference costs vs standard attention.

The related DeepSeek V3.2-Speciale model won gold at the International Mathematical Olympiad, International Olympiad in Informatics, and International Collegiate Programming Contest 2026 — the first AI to achieve this. The R1 reasoning model achieves 97.3% on MATH-500. This is not a company that benchmarks well by accident.

01
MLA Architecture & the 88% Inference Cost Reduction
// 22 marks
▼

DeepSeek V4 Pro's cost efficiency comes from Multi-head Latent Attention. Understanding MLA is the core of this assignment — not just what it achieves, but how.

MoE with 680B total / 42B active: Explain the Mixture-of-Experts architecture: how does the routing mechanism decide which of the 680B parameters to activate for a given token? What are the inference latency implications of 42B active parameters vs a dense 42B model that activates all parameters? Why does MoE at 680B not require 680B's worth of compute per token?
Multi-head Latent Attention (MLA) explained: What is MLA technically? How does it differ from standard Multi-Head Attention (MHA) and Group-Query Attention (GQA, used in Llama and GPT models)? What specific memory bottleneck in the KV cache does it address? How does it achieve 88% inference cost reduction? Explain the key equations in plain English.
Native thinking integrated with tool use: V4 Pro integrates reasoning with tool-use decisions — the model can reason about which tools to call and what arguments to pass as part of its thinking chain, not as a post-hoc decision. How does this differ architecturally from GPT-5.5's function calling? What quality improvement does integrated reasoning produce for multi-step tool-use tasks?
V4 Pro vs R1 vs R2: DeepSeek has V-series (general purpose) and R-series (dedicated reasoning) models. R1 achieves 97.3% on MATH-500. How does V4 Pro incorporate reasoning without being a pure reasoning model? When should you choose R1 over V4 Pro, and when is V4 Pro sufficient?
V3.2-Speciale competition performance: DeepSeek V3.2-Speciale won gold at IMO, IOI, and ICPC 2026. These are not benchmarks — they are live competitions with novel problems. What does winning these competitions demonstrate that benchmark scores cannot capture? What types of reasoning did the IMO, IOI, and ICPC 2026 problems specifically require?
