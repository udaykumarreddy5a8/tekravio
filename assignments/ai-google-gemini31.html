// The Context
Gemini 3.1 Pro arrived February 19, 2026. The .1 naming is deliberate: Google previously used .5 increments (Gemini 1.5, 2.5). The .1 signals a focused intelligence upgrade to the core reasoning system — not a sweeping architectural overhaul. The result: ARC-AGI-2 jumped from 31.1% to 77.1% in three months. GPQA Diamond reached 94.3%.

The model runs a Mixture-of-Experts architecture with three thinking tiers. It processes five modalities natively in one model: text, images, audio, video, and code. It can hold 8.4 hours of audio, 900-page PDFs, or 1 hour of video in a single context. Context caching can reduce costs by up to 75%. At $2/$12 per million tokens, it undercuts Claude Fable 5 by 5× on input.

01
Architecture: MoE, Three-Tier Thinking & the .1 Naming Choice
// 20 marks
▼

Gemini 3.1 Pro's architecture is distinct from GPT and Claude. Understanding MoE and the thinking tier system is foundational for every other module.

Why .1 not .5: Google used .5 increments for Gemini 1.5 and 2.5. The .1 change signals a focused reasoning upgrade. Document what specifically changed between Gemini 3 Pro and 3.1 Pro. What did not change? Why does this naming distinction matter for developers who upgraded from Gemini 3 Pro?
Mixture-of-Experts architecture: Explain MoE in practical terms: how does it route tokens to different expert sub-networks? What are the inference cost advantages over a dense transformer of equivalent total parameters? What are the trade-offs in output consistency and reproducibility across calls?
Three-tier thinking system: Low (fastest, classification), Medium (balanced — new in 3.1, code review and data analysis), High (maximum depth, complex research). How does the model decide which tier to apply if not specified? How does a developer force a specific tier via the API? When does Low tier save enough money that the quality trade-off is justified?
Native multimodal architecture: Gemini was built multimodal from the ground up — text, images, audio, video, and code as first-class inputs in one model. Contrast with GPT-5.5 (text/image, no native video) and Claude Fable 5 (text/image/PDF). What specific enterprise tasks can Gemini 3.1 Pro handle in a single API call that competitors require multiple calls or separate models for?
Context window practical implications: 1,048,576 input tokens (1M), 65,536 output. Documents it can ingest: 8.4 hours of audio, 900-page PDFs, or 1 hour of video in one prompt. Document 3 specific enterprise scenarios where this multimodal + context combination creates capabilities no other frontier model matches today.
02
ARC-AGI-2 at 77.1% — The Most Important Benchmark Story of Early 2026
// 22 marks
▼

Gemini 3.1 Pro's score on ARC-AGI-2 is the headline achievement of this model. It jumped from 31.1% (Gemini 3 Pro) to 77.1% in three months. Understand what this benchmark actually measures and what the score means.

The 31.1% → 77.1% jump: Gemini 3 Pro scored 31.1% in November 2025. Gemini 3.1 Pro scores 77.1% in February 2026. This is more than doubling in three months. What changed architecturally or in training to produce this jump? Is this improvement expected or surprising given the .1 increment?
GPQA Diamond at 94.3%: GPQA Diamond tests graduate-level scientific knowledge in chemistry, biology, and physics. Gemini 3.1 Pro scores 94.3% vs Claude Opus 4.6 at 92.8%. Is the 1.5-point gap meaningful in practice? For a researcher designing a literature review tool, what does this difference translate to in real research quality?

Deep Think mode: Google's extended-reasoning variant, gated to AI Ultra ($99.99+/mo). What does Deep Think add over the High thinking tier? Compare to Claude Fable 5's adaptive thinking. For a biomedical research application, when does the $99.99/mo Deep Think premium justify itself over High tier?
