// Context
The situation: Tekravio is growing fast. We have 400+ applications across Java, Python, React, Node.js, SDET, and now HR. We have no dedicated HR person. Applications are being reviewed by the founder between client meetings. The process is inconsistent, response times are slow, and some great candidates may have already dropped off.

Your mission: You're stepping in as HR Intern. Your job is to fix the process, create structure, and ensure every applicant gets a fair, timely, and honest experience — whether the answer is yes or no. This assignment simulates that work.

What Tekravio screens for (not GPA, not college): Genuine curiosity, mindset, what they've built, why Tekravio specifically.

// Core Tasks

5 tasks. 100 marks. 3 days.

Each task is a real piece of work you'd own as HR Intern at Tekravio. We evaluate quality of thinking, not length of output.

01
Write 2 Job Descriptions for Open Roles
// 20 marks

Write two complete, publish-ready JDs for Tekravio. One for a Java Backend Intern and one for an HR Intern (yes, your own role). These will be evaluated on clarity, honesty, tone, and whether a good candidate would actually apply after reading them.

Tone: Direct, honest, no corporate fluff. Tekravio's voice is "we don't care about your GPA, we care about what you've built." Match it.
Structure per JD: Role title, what you'll own (not "responsibilities"), what we're looking for (not "requirements"), what you get, and how to apply
Java JD: Must mention Spring Boot, real enterprise projects, PPO — without sounding like a Naukri template
HR JD: Must communicate what real HR ownership at a startup looks like — not filing and scheduling
Each JD: 200–350 words. Not a word more.
// What We're Really Testing

Can you write in a voice that isn't yours? Can you understand what a company actually values and translate it into words that attract the right people? A bland JD is an immediate fail regardless of how correct it is structurally.

02
Application Screening — Judge 5 Real Profiles
// 25 marks

Below are 5 fictional applicant profiles for the Java Backend Intern role. For each, write a screening verdict (Proceed / Hold / Reject) with a 3–5 sentence honest justification. Then write the actual response email you'd send them.

Profile A — Riya, 3rd year CSE, CGPA 9.1, no GitHub, no personal projects, cover letter says "I am hardworking and passionate about technology"
Profile B — Karan, 2nd year, CGPA 6.4, GitHub with 4 personal projects, built a Spring Boot food delivery API with JWT auth, cover letter mentions a specific problem he faced while building it
Profile C — Sneha, final year, CGPA 7.8, internship at TCS for 6 months ("worked on Java project"), no GitHub link, generic cover letter
Profile D — Arjun, dropped out of college, no formal degree, 2 open-source contributions to Spring projects, built a microservices side project deployed on AWS, mentions he learned everything from documentation and building
Profile E — Meera, MBA student applying for Java intern "because she wants to learn coding", no technical background, well-written thoughtful cover letter about why she wants to transition
// Grading Note

We are checking your judgment, not looking for a "correct" answer. What matters: can you see past the surface, recognise genuine effort, and communicate rejections with kindness and honesty? The email you write to Profile A matters as much as the verdict.

03
Design Tekravio's End-to-End Hiring Process
// 25 marks

Right now Tekravio's hiring process is: get application → review it whenever → send assignment → wait → do one interview → offer. There's no structure, no SLAs, no documentation. Design the full process.

Stage map: Every stage from "application received" to "offer sent" — what happens, who does it, how long it takes, what the candidate experiences at each step
SLAs: Define specific response time commitments. E.g. "Application acknowledged within X hours, screening verdict within Y days." Be realistic, not aspirational.
Rejection communication policy: How does Tekravio tell people they didn't get through? When? What does the message say? Design the template.
Assignment stage: How is the assignment shared? What instructions do candidates get? How is it tracked?
Interview stage: What does the 1 interview round cover for an intern? Who conducts it? What's the format? How long?
Offer stage: What does an offer communication look like? What information does it include?

Present this as a document — a structured process guide someone could follow on Day 1 without any context. Diagrams/flowcharts welcome. Plain text is fine if it's clear.

04
Write 3 Candidate Communication Templates
// 15 marks

Tekravio's candidate communication is currently inconsistent. Write three ready-to-use email templates that match Tekravio's direct, honest, human tone.

Template 1 — Application received + high volume delay notice: Acknowledge the application, mention the delay honestly, set clear expectations. Based on the real situation: 400+ applications received.
Template 2 — Assignment share: Candidate has passed screening. Sharing the assignment link, instructions, deadline. Warm but clear. No corporate jargon.
Template 3 — Rejection after assignment review: The candidate submitted but didn't make it to the interview round. Honest, kind, specific enough to be useful. Not "we'll keep your profile on file."
// Common Failure Mode

Most candidates write templates that sound like they were copied from a corporate HR manual. Tekravio's voice is human. If Uday wouldn't say it in a WhatsApp message, it doesn't belong in the email.

05
Employer Branding — 1 LinkedIn Post Draft
// 15 marks

Write one LinkedIn post from Uday's perspective about hiring at Tekravio. Not a job advertisement. A genuine, first-person post that builds employer brand and makes the right kind of candidates want to apply.

Length: 200–350 words. Written as Uday, first person.
Angle — pick one: (a) What Tekravio looks for in candidates that most companies miss, (b) What the hiring process looks like from the inside, (c) Why Tekravio rejects CVs with a 9+ CGPA but no GitHub
Tone: Honest, direct, slightly contrarian. Not a hype post. No "excited to announce" energy.
CTA: Subtle — not "apply now!!!" at the end.
Hashtags: 4–6 relevant ones. Don't pad.

// Mindset Questions

Answer These in Your Submission Doc

// Q1
You've screened 50 applications. 30 are clear rejects. 12 are maybe. 8 are strong. The founder only has time to review 5 this week. How do you decide which 5? What's your decision framework?
// Q2
A candidate who was rejected 3 months ago emails asking for feedback. They mention they've been improving and reapplied. They're borderline on the new application. How do you handle both requests — the feedback ask and the new application?
// Q3
Tekravio says it doesn't filter on CGPA or college name. But Uday subconsciously tends to look more favourably at candidates from known colleges. As the HR intern, how do you address this — practically, not ideally?
// Q4
You receive an application that's technically weak but has a cover letter that shows exceptional self-awareness and a genuine story. You believe in them but can't technically justify it. Do you advance them? How do you defend that decision to the founder?
