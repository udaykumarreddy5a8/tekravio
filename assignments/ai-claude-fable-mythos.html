// Why These Models Matter
Mythos-class models sit above the Opus class in Anthropic's model hierarchy. Fable is from the Latin fabula — "that which is told" — akin to the Greek mythos. 02
Fable 5 vs Mythos 5 — Same Weights, Different Rules
// 14 marks · The core distinction
▼

Fable 5 and Mythos 5 are the same underlying model. What separates them is entirely about safeguards and access. Document this distinction with precision.

✖
Identical specs table: Build a complete side-by-side table of Fable 5 vs Mythos 5 across every available dimension: API model ID, context window, max output tokens, pricing (input + output per million tokens), adaptive thinking, data retention policy, availability, and safety classifiers. Make it copy-paste ready for a client slide.
✖
The classifier difference explained: Fable 5 has safety classifiers active; Mythos 5 does not. What are these classifiers? How do they work technically? When a request triggers a Fable 5 classifier, what happens — exactly? What does the API return (hint: stop_reason: "refusal")? What does the user experience look like?
✖
Fallback mechanics: When Fable 5 refuses a request, it falls back to Claude Opus 4.8. Document the three fallback options in the API: server-side fallback (via the fallbacks parameter), client-side fallback (SDK middleware), and manual retry. What is "fallback credit" and how does billing work when a request falls back?
✖
The 5% figure: Anthropic states that classifiers trigger in fewer than 5% of Fable 5 sessions on average. What does this mean in practice? For a company running 10,000 API requests/day, estimate how many fallbacks they should plan for. How should they architect their application to handle this gracefully?
✖
Adaptive Thinking — always on: Fable 5 and Mythos 5 have adaptive thinking always enabled. What is adaptive thinking? How does it differ from the thinking mode on earlier Claude models? What does thinking.display: "summarized" vs "omitted" mean, and when would you use each in production?
✖
Raw thinking never returned: Fable 5 and Mythos 5 never return the raw chain of thought, only a summary or empty field. Why did Anthropic make this design choice for Mythos-class models specifically? What are the implications for debugging and evaluation?
✖
Data retention: Both models carry a 30-day data retention requirement and are classified as "Covered Models" — they are not available under zero data retention. Document what this means. Which enterprise customers would this be a blocking issue for, and what are their options?

The Safety Architecture — How Fable 5 Is Made Safe for General Use
// 16 marks · The safeguard deep dive
▼

Fable 5's safety classifier system is a genuine technical innovation — not just a content policy update. Understand and document how it works, what it blocks, what it doesn't block, and why.

✖
What are safety classifiers? Explain classifiers as a concept: separate AI systems that run alongside the main model, detect potential misuse (including jailbreak attempts), and intercept before the main model responds. How is this different from the in-model refusal training used in earlier Claude models? What is the architectural advantage?
✖
The three blocked domains: Document each of the three domains covered by Fable 5's classifiers with full detail:

1. Cybersecurity: Mythos-class models excel at finding and exploiting software vulnerabilities, agentic hacking (reconnaissance, lateral movement, exploitation as a complete attack chain). How do the classifiers prevent this? What is the Firefox metric referenced in Anthropic's announcement? Include the actual metric they use to demonstrate classifier effectiveness.

2. Biology & Chemistry: What specific bio/chem capabilities triggered the decision to classify this domain? What types of queries fall back to Opus 4.8?

3. Distillation: What is model distillation in this context? Why is it a concern with Mythos-class models specifically?
✖
Jailbreak resistance: Anthropic says internal and external red-teaming found no "universal jailbreaks" after more than 1,000 hours of testing, and that more than 95% of Fable 5 sessions run on Fable itself with no fallback. Document what this means for security. Why is jailbreak resistance specifically important for Mythos-class models (think: what's the incentive for adversarial actors)?
✖
False positives — the known limitation: Anthropic acknowledges the classifiers are "deliberately more conservative" and will sometimes catch harmless requests. This is a known trade-off at launch. Document: what does a false positive look like for a legitimate user? What Anthropic says about reducing false positives over time? For which professional domains (cybersecurity researchers, biology researchers) is this friction most significant?
✖
Constitutional AI and the classifier lineage: How do Fable 5's classifiers relate to Anthropic's earlier next-generation constitutional classifier work? Document what this earlier research established and what Fable 5's classifiers add on top of it.
✖
Alignment assessment: What did Anthropic's automated alignment assessment find about Mythos 5's level of misaligned behaviour (deception, cooperation with misuse)? How does it compare to Opus 4.8? What does it mean that alignment is "similar" even though Mythos-class is dramatically more capable?
// The Dual-Use Problem
✖
Write a 200-word analysis of the dual-use problem as it applies to Mythos-class models. The same query that helps a cybersecurity professional find a vulnerability could help a criminal exploit it. How does Anthropic's approach balance utility for legitimate use cases against misuse prevention? Where does the line get drawn, and who decides?
